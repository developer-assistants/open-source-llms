Frequently asked questions

What is the best open source LLM right now?

On multi-GPU servers, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3.1, Qwen3-235B-A22B, and Qwen3-Coder-480B lead on reasoning and software engineering under MIT and Apache 2.0 terms. On a single 24 GB GPU, Qwen3-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, Devstral Small 24B, and Gemma 3 27B give the highest output quality without cluster hardware.

Which free large language models allow unrestricted commercial use?

Models published under Apache 2.0 or MIT let you host, fine-tune, and ship commercial products without active-user ceilings. That group includes DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3.1, the Qwen3 line, Mistral Small 3.1, Devstral Small, GLM-4.5, gpt-oss-120b, gpt-oss-20b, Phi-4, and OLMo 2.

How much VRAM do I need to run an open source LLM locally?

At 4-bit quantization, an 8B dense model takes 5 to 6 GB of VRAM, a 14B model takes around 10 GB, a 24B to 32B model fits inside 16 to 24 GB, and a 70B dense model needs about 42 GB before KV cache. Leave 2 to 6 GB of spare memory if you plan to use 32K or 128K context windows.

What is the difference between open source LLMs and open-weight models?

Most releases in this directory are open-weight: the weights and inference code are free to download, while the pretraining corpus stays private. Fully open-source releases such as AllenAI OLMo 2 also publish the training datasets, data-filtering scripts, and intermediate checkpoints under Apache 2.0.
